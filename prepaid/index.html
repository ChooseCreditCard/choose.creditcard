Prepaid Cards vs Credit Cards

Prepaid cards look like normal cards, but you can only spend money you’ve already loaded. This guide explains how they differ from debit and credit cards – and when they are useful.

General information only. Products and rules vary by country and provider.

What is a prepaid card?

Basic model

You load money onto the card first, then spend from that balance. There is no credit line, and you normally can’t go into negative.

Who uses them?

  • People who want strict budgeting tools
  • Parents setting limits for teens
  • Travellers using separate travel balances
  • People who can’t or don’t want traditional credit lines

Prepaid vs debit vs credit

Type Source of money Credit line?
Prepaid Loaded balance on the card No
Debit Your bank account balance Usually no (except overdraft)
Credit Borrowed from lender up to a limit Yes

Fees and limitations to check

  • Top-up fees when loading money onto the card.
  • Monthly or inactivity fees.
  • ATM withdrawal fees, especially abroad.
  • Foreign currency fees on international use.
  • Limits on maximum balance or monthly loading.

When a prepaid card is a good fit

  • You want to avoid the risk of debt or overspending.
  • You prefer to separate specific budgets (e.g. travel, gifts, allowance).
  • You want card-like convenience without applying for credit.
  • You are teaching someone how to handle card payments safely.

Prepaid cards typically do not build credit history, because there is no borrowing involved.

Compare cards and payment options

If you want to keep things simple, a no-fee or low-limit credit card – or a strong debit solution – might also be worth comparing alongside prepaid products.
