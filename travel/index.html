without getting surprised abroad.

This sub-hub on Choose.Creditcard is focused on cards used outside your home country: foreign exchange fees, ATM withdrawals, airport lounges, and bundled travel insurance. Everything here is a prototype and educational only.

When this type of card makes sense

  • You travel internationally more than 1–2 times per year.
  • You spend a lot in foreign currencies online or in physical stores.
  • You care about airport lounge access, priority boarding or hotel perks.
  • You want clear, documented travel insurance rather than “maybe covered”.

Travel & FX card FAQ (prototype)

What is a travel credit card?

A travel credit card is a card that is optimised for spending outside your home country. The key features are foreign exchange (FX) fees, ATM fees abroad, and whether the card includes documented travel insurance and airport lounge access.

What should I look for in a travel card with no foreign fees?

Look for 0% FX fees on purchases, clear ATM terms abroad, and transparent travel insurance. A “no foreign fees” card with poor insurance or high ATM markups can still be expensive on trips.

Do airport lounges always make a travel card worth it?

Not necessarily. Lounge access only makes sense if you actually fly often enough to use it. Occasional travellers are often better off with a cheaper travel card with low FX fees.
