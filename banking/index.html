Banking & Everyday Payment Cards

Most people use a mix of debit and credit cards connected to their main bank. This page explains how those cards link to your account, and what to watch in fees and overdrafts.

This is a general guide. For specific products and offers, use our comparison tool.

Main card types in everyday banking

Debit card

Money is taken directly from your current account. Good for day-to-day spending if you avoid overdrafts.

Credit card

You borrow from the card issuer and pay later, usually with an interest-free period if you pay in full.

Bank card with overdraft

A debit card linked to an account that can go below zero, often with interest and fees.

How cards connect to your bank account

Debit card: every transaction hits your current account directly. Balance changes immediately or within a short time.

Credit card: transactions collect on a separate statement. You repay from your bank account on the due date.

Linked payment apps / wallets: Apple Pay, Google Pay and similar still route the money via your card (debit or credit).

Debit, credit and overdraft – quick comparison

Feature Debit card Credit card Overdraft on account
Where the money sits Your current account Separate credit line Current account, below zero
Interest cost None, if no overdraft If you don’t pay statement in full If your balance is negative
Typical use Daily payments, cash withdrawals Travel, online shopping, rewards Short-term buffer, should be used with care

What to watch in everyday banking cards

  • Fees for ATM withdrawals (at home and abroad).
  • Foreign transaction fees when you travel.
  • Interest and fees on overdrafts or unpaid credit card balances.
  • Daily or monthly limits that might block larger purchases.
  • How easy it is to freeze, replace or manage cards in the app.
