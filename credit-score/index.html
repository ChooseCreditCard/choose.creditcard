How credit scores are calculated

Credit score models differ between countries, but most include the same core ideas: payment history, balances, length of credit, new inquiries and account mix.

Payment history

The most important factor. Paying on time builds trust with lenders.

Late payments can stay on your record for years.

Credit utilization

How much of your limit you use. Lower is usually better.

Often recommended: below 30%, ideally below 10%.

Length of credit history

Older accounts show long-term, stable behaviour.

Closing old accounts may affect this factor.

New credit checks

Many applications in a short time can lower your score.

Soft checks usually don’t affect it.

Account mix

A mix of loans, credit cards and instalments can help a score.

Only relevant once you have basic history.

Consistency over time

The score improves slowly through positive behaviour.

There are no shortcuts to repairing credit.
