Secured & rebuild credit cards
— building history the safe way.

Secured cards exist for one reason: to give banks a way to approve people without a strong credit file. You pay a deposit, which becomes your credit limit. You use the card normally, and your payments are reported to the credit bureaus.

This guide explains how secured cards work, how long they should be used, and how to transition to unsecured credit.

What makes a card “secured”?

A secured card requires a refundable deposit — typically €100 to €500 — which acts as collateral. This reduces risk for the bank, allowing approvals for:

  • Newcomers with no previous credit history
  • Students or young adults
  • People rebuilding after missed payments or defaults
  • People with unstable income history

The bank does not use your deposit for spending — it is held aside and returned when you upgrade.

How secured cards help you build credit

Secured cards report to the main credit bureaus just like standard cards. Consistent on-time payments demonstrate responsible usage. Within 6–18 months, most users can transition to an unsecured product.

1. On-time payments

Payment history is usually the biggest scoring factor. One clean line helps a lot.

2. Low utilization

Keeping usage under 10–30% of your limit shows discipline and reduces perceived risk.

3. Account age

The account becomes part of your “average age” once it graduates to an unsecured card.

Important: A secured card only helps if you avoid carrying a balance and avoid late payments.

How long should you keep a secured card?

Most people use secured cards for 6–18 months. The goal is not to stay secured forever, but to:

  • Build a clean payment history
  • Demonstrate stable, predictable usage
  • Trigger an automatic upgrade to unsecured
  • Get your deposit refunded

Some issuers automatically review accounts; others require a manual request.
