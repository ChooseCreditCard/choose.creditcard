Guides · Travel & foreign spending

Using credit cards abroad
— FX fees, DCC and ATM withdrawals.

This guide explains, in neutral language, how credit cards typically behave when you travel: foreign exchange (FX) markups, “no foreign fee” marketing, dynamic currency conversion at terminals, and common ATM fee structures. It is not personal financial advice and not tied to any specific issuer or country.

Always check the card’s own price list and your local bank or issuer documentation for exact terms.

How card FX fees usually work

When you pay in a foreign currency, most card networks use a wholesale exchange rate for that day and the issuer may add an extra percentage on top, often called an FX fee or foreign transaction fee. Typical ingredients include:

  • Base rate – network wholesale rate (e.g. Visa/Mastercard).
  • Issuer markup – a percentage added by the bank or card issuer.
  • Additional service fees – sometimes on cash withdrawals or special products.

A card marketed as “no foreign fees” often means the issuer markup is 0%, but the underlying network rate can still move from day to day.

Dynamic currency conversion (DCC) at terminals

Many terminals and ATMs offer to charge you in your “home currency” instead of the local currency. This is often called dynamic currency conversion (DCC). It can feel convenient, but the rate is usually set by the terminal provider, not your card network.

Local currency

Transaction goes through the card network with standard wholesale rate + issuer FX fee.

Home currency via DCC

The terminal provider sets the rate and markup. FX fee from issuer may or may not apply.

What to compare

Look at the effective rate shown on screen, not just the label. Markups can be several percent.

Whether DCC is sensible depends on your card’s FX terms and the offered rate. This guide does not give a blanket recommendation – it only explains the structure.

ATMs, cash withdrawals and extra fees

Withdrawing cash on a credit card can trigger several layered charges. Typical examples:

  • Cash advance fee from the issuer (fixed or percentage-based).
  • Interest that starts earlier than on normal purchases in some markets.
  • ATM operator fees, sometimes displayed on-screen before you accept.
  • Standard FX markup if you withdraw in a foreign currency.

Some products are designed for travel cash withdrawals with lower or capped fees, others are clearly not. Always read the card’s own price list before relying on it for cash abroad.
