Secured Credit Cards

A secured credit card requires a refundable security deposit. It’s one of the most reliable ways to build or rebuild credit — as long as you use it responsibly.

General information only. Terms vary across banks and countries.

What is a secured credit card?

Security deposit

You place a cash deposit (often $100–$300). The bank uses this as collateral and usually sets your credit limit equal to the deposit.

Purpose

Secured cards help people with low or no credit history access a credit line. Payments are reported to credit bureaus, helping build positive history.

Benefits of a secured card

  • Easier approval compared to unsecured cards.
  • Predictable spending limits.
  • Builds payment history month by month.
  • Deposit is refundable when the card is upgraded or closed responsibly.

Things to watch out for

  • Some secured cards still charge annual fees.
  • Interest rates can be higher than standard cards.
  • Missing payments still harms your credit score.
  • Your deposit may be held for months after closing if there is unresolved debt.

When a secured card is a good idea

  • You have limited or damaged credit history.
  • You want to build a positive payment record.
  • You prefer a predictable, low-limit starter card.
  • You can afford the upfront refundable deposit.

For some users, a student card or low-fee card may also work — depending on income and credit profile.
