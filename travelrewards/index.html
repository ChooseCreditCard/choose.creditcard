Travel Rewards Credit Cards

Travel rewards cards can give you points, miles, lounge access and insurance – but only if the perks match how you actually travel. This page gives you a clean overview before you choose.

General information only. Products and conditions vary between banks and countries.

What is a travel rewards card?

Core idea

A travel rewards card earns extra value when you travel — through points, miles, no-foreign-fee terms, lounge access or bundled travel insurance.

Typical perks

  • Extra points or miles on travel spend
  • Airport lounge access or priority services
  • Travel insurance (trip, baggage, delay)
  • Reduced or zero foreign transaction fees

Key factors to compare

Factor Why it matters
Foreign transaction fees Can quietly eat into every purchase abroad.
Earnings on travel Extra rewards on flights, hotels or transport increase value.
Lounge access Useful if you fly often and value airport comfort.
Insurance coverage May replace or reduce the need for separate travel insurance.

Points, miles and partner programs

Travel cards often connect to airline or hotel programs, or to a flexible bank points system.

  • Airline miles: best value for frequent flyers on a specific airline group.
  • Hotel points: strongest if you often stay in the same chain.
  • Flexible bank points: more choice between airlines, hotels and cashback.

When a travel rewards card makes sense

  • You travel a few times per year or more.
  • You pay your card in full and avoid interest.
  • You value lounge access or insurance enough to justify any annual fee.
  • Your normal spending fits the card’s bonus categories.

If you rarely travel or often carry a balance, a simpler low-fee or cashback card may be better.
