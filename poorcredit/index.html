Credit Cards for Poor Credit

Having poor credit doesn’t mean you are stuck forever, but it does mean you need to be extra careful with new credit. This page explains common options – and the traps to avoid.

General information only. Products, rules and credit scoring models differ by country.

Main options if you have poor credit

Secured cards

You pay a deposit, get a small limit, and use the card to build new history.

Credit builder cards

Designed for low scores, often with lower limits but higher interest.

No new card

Sometimes the best move is to stabilise existing debts first.

What to expect from “poor credit” cards

Aspect Typical reality
Credit limit Lower than standard cards, often a few hundred.
Interest (APR) Frequently higher – carrying a balance is expensive.
Rewards Usually limited or none; focus is on rebuilding, not perks.
Fees Can include annual fees, setup fees or other charges – always read the fee table.

Traps to avoid

  • Cards marketed aggressively to people in financial stress, with very high fees.
  • Carrying a large balance at high interest “just to build credit faster”.
  • Using multiple “poor credit” cards at once to access more spending.
  • Ignoring existing debts while opening new lines of credit.

Improving credit is usually about stability over time – not taking on more expensive debt.

Using a card to improve poor credit

  • Start with one card only, preferably simple and transparent.
  • Use it for small, predictable expenses you can always repay.
  • Pay at least the statement balance on time every month (full balance is best).
  • Avoid maxing out the limit; keep usage moderate.
  • Track your credit report and score over time.
