Student Credit Cards

Student cards are built for people with limited credit history. Used correctly, they can help you build a track record. Used badly, they can create expensive debt early in life.

General information only. Rules, age limits and products vary by country and bank.

What is a student credit card?

Basic concept

A student credit card is designed for people in education, with lower limits and simpler reward structures, often with fewer or no annual fees.

Typical features

  • Lower credit limits
  • Basic cashback or no rewards
  • Lower or waived annual fee
  • Requirements such as proof of study or co-signer (in some markets)

Student vs regular credit cards

Aspect Student card Standard card
Limit Lower, easier to control Higher, based on income/credit score
Fees Often low or no annual fee Varies a lot, some premium tiers
Rewards Basic or none More options, higher potential value

How to use a student card safely

  • Start with small, predictable expenses (e.g. a streaming service or phone bill).
  • Pay the full balance on time every month – aim for 0 in carried interest.
  • Avoid using the card as an “extra income source”.
  • Turn on notifications in the app so you see each transaction.
  • Stay away from cash withdrawals if they trigger extra fees or interest.

Building a credit history

In some countries, using a credit card responsibly can help build your credit record over time. This usually means:

  • Making at least the minimum payment on time (ideally the full amount).
  • Keeping utilisation moderate compared to your limit.
  • Avoiding late payments and over-limit situations.

How credit scores work depends on the country. Check local rules or official guidance for details.
